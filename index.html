Government Should Work for the People.

Janavani is a privacy-first citizen governance platform being built to help people understand government systems, exercise their rights, take informed civic action and participate in accountable governance.

What Is Janavani?

Janavani means the voice of the people.

Janavani is being developed as a citizen-governance platform that helps people move from a government-related problem to informed, documented and lawful civic action.

Government systems can be complicated. Citizens should not be expected to understand every department, office, procedure, document or escalation pathway before they can exercise their rights.

Janavani aims to carry that complexity while keeping the citizen at the centre of the process.

What Janavani Is Building Today

The current product is focused on making government interaction simpler, more structured and more useful.

Describe a Problem

Citizens can describe a government-related problem in ordinary language without first understanding the entire administrative system.

Identify the Pathway

Janavani is designed to help identify the relevant authority, office, document and procedural pathway.

Prepare Documents

Citizens can work toward structured, government-facing documents that they can review before using them.

Preserve Evidence

The platform is designed to support evidence-based civic action using relevant documents, records, photographs and information.

Follow Up

Future workflows can help citizens track responses, follow up and understand appropriate escalation pathways.

Privacy First

Janavani is being designed around privacy, security, data minimisation and citizen control.

Democracy Is More Than Protest

In a democracy and republic, citizens have many ways to express disagreement, seek accountability and participate in public affairs.

Protest is one form of democratic participation. It is not the only form.

Citizens can also communicate through properly structured representations, petitions, requests for information, complaints, objections, legal notices and other lawful procedures available to them.

Where permitted and appropriate, citizens may also explore party-in-person procedures rather than depending on a lawyer for every stage.

Janavani aims to make these pathways easier to understand and navigate while encouraging lawful, evidence-based and responsible civic participation.

A Citizen Has More Than One Path

Representation

Communicate a problem, request or concern to the appropriate government authority in a structured written form.

RTI

Where appropriate, seek information through the Right to Information framework and understand response and appeal pathways.

Objection & Opinion

Respond to proposed policies, laws and public decisions through reasoned opinions, objections and representations.

Legal Notice

Where appropriate, citizens may communicate legal concerns formally before considering further legal action.

Party-in-Person

Citizens may explore available party-in-person procedures where permitted and appropriate.

Community Support

Citizens may seek assistance from civil-society, legal-aid and other organisations working in relevant areas.

Toward Participatory Governance

These are long-term directions for Janavani. They are not claims about current availability.

Government Performance

Help citizens understand government commitments, programmes, implementation and measurable outcomes.

Manifesto & Performance

Explore ways to compare publicly available election commitments with government performance at appropriate levels.

Budget & Outcomes

Help citizens understand public allocations and, where reliable data is available, compare spending with implementation and outcomes.

Elected Representatives

Help citizens understand publicly available information about MPs, MLAs and local representatives, including participation and legislative activity.

Government Offices

Develop structured citizen feedback about service experience, accessibility, responsiveness and public-office conditions.

Citizen Ratings

Explore evidence-based citizen feedback and rating systems with safeguards against abuse, manipulation and unverified allegations.

Public Servants

Create responsible ways for citizens to recognise professionalism, responsiveness and public service.

Public Accountability

Build reliable public information from verified sources, documented evidence and transparent verification processes.

Constitutional Awareness

Janavani aims to help citizens better understand the constitutional framework within which government power operates.

An important part of Indian constitutional jurisprudence is the interrelationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21, commonly described as the constitutional “Golden Triangle”.

Janavani may use constitutional principles as part of future educational and civic-information tools while making clear that such information does not replace professional legal advice or judicial determination.

Privacy Is Not an Afterthought

Citizens should not have to surrender unnecessary personal information simply to participate in civic life.

Janavani is therefore being designed around privacy, security, data minimisation and citizen control.

The long-term research direction includes stronger privacy-preserving technologies and decentralised infrastructure that may improve the resilience and integrity of citizen information.

These advanced capabilities belong to the long-term development direction and are not represented as current production features.

Citizens Are Not Just Users

Citizens, developers, designers, researchers, legal professionals, governance experts, journalists, students, civil-society organisations and volunteers can contribute in different ways.

Citizens

Share real-world civic problems, experiences and ideas that can help improve the platform.

Developers

Contribute code, testing, security research, architecture and open-source development.

Researchers

Contribute research in governance, law, public administration, technology, privacy and democracy.

Legal Professionals

Help improve legal accuracy, document structures, procedural information and civic legal resources.

Designers & Communicators

Help make complex government processes easier for ordinary citizens to understand.

Organisations

Civil-society organisations and community groups can explore research, awareness and collaboration.
