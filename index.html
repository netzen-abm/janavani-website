Government Should Work for the People.

Janavani is a privacy-first citizen governance platform being developed to help people understand government systems, exercise their rights, take informed civic action and participate in accountable governance.

See How It Works Get Involved

What Is Janavani?

Janavani means the voice of the people.

Janavani is being developed as a citizen-governance platform that helps people move from a government-related problem to informed, documented and lawful civic action.

Government systems can be complicated. Citizens should not have to understand every department, office, procedure, document or escalation pathway before they can exercise their rights.

Janavani aims to carry that complexity while keeping the citizen at the centre of the process.

Who Is Janavani For?

Janavani is designed around ordinary citizens and the people who help build better civic systems.

Citizens

People who need to understand a government problem, identify a pathway, prepare a document or follow up on an unresolved matter.

Communities

Groups of citizens dealing with common public-service, administrative or civic issues.

Researchers

People working on law, governance, public administration, democracy, technology, privacy and public policy.

Contributors

Developers, designers, legal professionals, students, journalists, volunteers and organisations that want to contribute to the project.

What Janavani Is Building Today

The immediate focus is a reliable citizen workflow: from a problem to a government-ready document.

Describe a Problem

Describe a government-related problem in ordinary language without first understanding the entire administrative structure.

Identify the Pathway

Structure the issue and work toward identifying the relevant authority, office, document and procedure.

Prepare Documents

Work toward structured government-facing documents that citizens can review before using them.

Preserve Evidence

Build evidence-based civic action using relevant records, documents, photographs and information.

Take Action

Help citizens understand possible lawful channels for submitting representations, complaints, information requests or other documents.

Privacy First

Janavani is being designed around privacy, data minimisation, security and citizen control.

How Janavani Works

The core idea is simple: the citizen explains the problem; Janavani helps organise the complexity.

1

Describe

Tell Janavani what happened in your own words.

2

Understand

Identify the relevant authority, issue category, procedure and possible civic pathway.

3

Prepare

Select or prepare the appropriate document and review the information before using it.

4

Act

Submit through an appropriate lawful channel, retain records and follow up when necessary.

Democracy Is More Than Protest

In a democracy and republic, citizens have many ways to express disagreement, seek accountability and participate in public affairs.

Protest is one form of democratic participation. It is not the only form.

Citizens can also communicate through properly structured representations, petitions, requests for information, complaints, objections, legal notices and other lawful procedures available to them.

A well-reasoned written representation can sometimes be a more direct and constructive way to place a problem, demand or objection before the appropriate authority.

Where permitted and appropriate, citizens may also explore party-in-person procedures rather than depending on a lawyer for every stage.

Janavani does not promote confrontation for its own sake. It aims to help citizens understand their available lawful choices and act with evidence, clarity and responsibility.

A Citizen Has More Than One Path

Representation

Communicate a problem, request or concern to the appropriate government authority in structured written form.

RTI

Where appropriate, seek information through the Right to Information framework and understand response and appeal pathways.

Petition

Present a structured request, grievance or public concern through an appropriate institutional pathway.

Objection & Opinion

Respond to proposed policies, laws and public decisions through reasoned opinions, objections and representations.

Legal Notice

Where appropriate, communicate a legal concern formally before considering further legal action.

Party-in-Person

Explore available party-in-person procedures where permitted, appropriate and practically possible.

Community Support

Citizens may seek assistance from civil-society, legal-aid and other organisations working in relevant areas.

Escalation

Where an appropriate response is not received, citizens may explore available follow-up, appeal or escalation mechanisms.

Constitutional Awareness

Janavani aims to help citizens better understand the constitutional framework within which government power operates.

An important part of Indian constitutional jurisprudence is the interrelationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21, commonly described as the constitutional "Golden Triangle".

Janavani may use constitutional principles as part of future educational and civic-information tools while making clear that such information does not replace professional legal advice or judicial determination.

Privacy Is Not an Afterthought

Citizens should not have to surrender unnecessary personal information simply to participate in civic life.

Janavani is therefore being designed around privacy, security, data minimisation and citizen control.

The long-term research direction includes stronger privacy-preserving technologies and decentralised infrastructure that may improve the resilience and integrity of citizen information.

These advanced capabilities belong to the long-term development direction and are not represented as current production features.
