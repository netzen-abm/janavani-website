How Janavani Works

Janavani is being built to help citizens move from a government-related problem to informed, documented and lawful civic action.

The Basic Idea

Citizens should describe their problem. Janavani should carry the complexity.

A citizen may know what happened without knowing which department, office, form or procedure is responsible.

Janavani is designed to progressively help structure that problem, identify possible pathways and prepare useful civic documents.

The objective is not to make citizens dependent on Janavani. The objective is to increase citizen understanding, agency and ability to act.

Current Janavani Workflow

The current MVP journey is designed around a simple citizen-to-document workflow.

1

Describe

Describe what happened in your own words. You do not need to understand the entire government structure first.

2

Select the Purpose

Identify the type of document or civic action that may be appropriate for the situation.

3

Provide Location

Provide the relevant district or location so Janavani can work toward identifying the appropriate government office.

4

Identify the Office

Search for the relevant government office using the available office information.

5

Choose Identity Mode

Provide the information required for the selected document and review what will be included.

6

Review & Generate

Review the prepared document before generating the final document for use.

7

Receive the Document

Generate and receive the document in the currently supported format.

8

Take Action

The citizen decides how and where to submit the document using the appropriate official channel.

Example: A Government Service Problem

A simplified example of the intended Janavani experience.

1. The Problem

A citizen has submitted an application for a government service but has not received the expected response.

2. Describe

The citizen explains what happened without needing to know the exact department or administrative hierarchy.

3. Understand

Janavani helps structure the issue and work toward identifying the appropriate authority and civic pathway.

4. Prepare

A structured representation or complaint can be prepared for the citizen to review.

5. Act

The citizen reviews the document and submits it through the appropriate official channel.

6. Follow Up

Future Janavani capabilities may help the citizen record responses and determine appropriate follow-up.

Action Does Not Mean Only Protest

Democracy and republican government provide citizens with many lawful ways to express disagreement, request accountability and participate in public affairs.

Depending on the situation, a citizen may use a representation, complaint, request for information, objection, petition, legal notice or another available administrative or legal pathway.

Where legally permitted, citizens may also explore party-in-person procedures and represent themselves in appropriate proceedings.

Janavani does not tell citizens which method they must use. It aims to help them understand available options so they can make informed decisions.

Party-in-Person & Community Support

Some citizens may choose to pursue matters personally through available legal procedures instead of relying entirely on professional representation.

The availability and procedure for party-in-person representation depends on the relevant court, authority, jurisdiction and type of proceeding.

Citizens should independently verify the applicable rules before proceeding.

Civil-society organisations, legal-aid initiatives and community groups may also provide assistance in some circumstances.

Janavani may provide references to such resources for informational purposes. Listing an organisation does not constitute endorsement by Janavani.

Current Capability vs Future Direction

Janavani's public website should never confuse planned capabilities with features that are already available.

Available / Current

The current platform includes the core conversation, workflow, office-selection, document-generation and PDF-delivery pathway being developed for the MVP.

Being Developed

Web-based citizen workflows, improved testing, reliability, privacy controls and broader document capabilities are part of the development roadmap.

Future

Tracking, evidence management, follow-up, escalation support, citizen feedback and broader governance intelligence are longer-term directions.

From Documents to Governance Intelligence

The document is not necessarily the end of the journey.

Evidence

Future workflows may help citizens organise photographs, records, documents and other relevant evidence.

Tracking

Future capabilities may help citizens record submissions, responses and important dates.

Follow-Up

Citizens may eventually receive structured guidance about possible next steps based on the response received.

Accountability

Aggregated and responsibly verified information could eventually help identify patterns in public-service delivery.

Public Learning

Reliable civic information can help communities understand recurring governance problems and possible solutions.

Participatory Governance

The long-term objective is to strengthen the ability of citizens to participate meaningfully in governance.

Privacy Is Part of the Design

Janavani is designed around privacy, security, data minimisation and citizen control.

Citizens should not have to provide unnecessary personal information simply to understand a government process or explore a civic pathway.

The platform architecture is intended to keep citizen capabilities independent from any particular interface such as Telegram or the Web.

Important Notice

Janavani is a civic-technology initiative. Information provided through the project is intended to support citizen understanding and participation.

Janavani does not replace a lawyer, court, government authority or other competent professional.

Laws, procedures, deadlines and official requirements should be independently verified before taking legal or administrative action.
