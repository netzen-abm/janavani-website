02 Â· The distinction

Assistance must not become authority.

A healthy civic system can help a person understand options and prepare action while leaving institutional authority, legal determination, and citizen judgment where they belong.

WHAT TECHNOLOGY CAN DO

Clarify the pathway.

Organise information, identify likely pathways, surface questions, structure a citizen's concern, and help preserve a useful record of what is known and unknown.

WHAT TECHNOLOGY MUST NOT DO

Replace the citizen or institution.

It should not pretend to possess public authority, manufacture certainty, decide institutional responsibility, or turn an automated answer into a substitute for an official process.

03 Â· Citizen capacity

The measure of success is what the citizen can do next.

A useful civic infrastructure should leave people with greater understanding, better records, clearer choices, and stronger confidence to participate without it.

05 Â· Development direction

Useful today. More capable tomorrow.

Janavani should grow from demonstrated civic usefulness, not from the assumption that more technology automatically means better governance.

NEAR TERM

Make difficult pathways understandable.

Start with concrete citizen problems where structured information and careful guidance can remove avoidable confusion.

CAPABILITY

Deepen civic intelligence responsibly.

Improve retrieval, classification, pathway guidance, records, and feedback while keeping source and uncertainty visible.

LARGER DIRECTION

Strengthen the civic fabric.

Build tools and practices that help people communicate, participate, learn from public processes, and build collective capacity.

06 Â· What the vision protects

The boundaries are part of the vision.

Citizen judgment

People should understand what a system recommends and retain the ability to accept, reject, question, or seek further confirmation.

Institutional responsibility

Public bodies remain responsible for public decisions. Janavani can help citizens navigate a process; it cannot become the authority that process belongs to.

Evidence and privacy

Civic participation depends on useful records, but the collection and handling of information must remain proportionate, purposeful, and responsible.

Human dignity

Every interaction should treat the citizen as a participant in a Republic, not as a case number to be processed.

The larger vision

Strengthen the citizen's capacity to understand, participate, communicate, and act.

That is the direction: not technology for its own sake, but civic infrastructure that helps the Republic work better for the people who constitute it.
