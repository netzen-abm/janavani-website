# JANAVANI — 12-PRIVACY-AND-CITIZEN-AGENCY

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the master privacy, contact-safety, AI-assistance, and citizen-agency principles for Janavani.

It establishes how Janavani must protect citizens from unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information while preserving the fundamental principle that technology assists citizens rather than replacing citizen judgment, lawful authority, or official determination.

This document is a content and implementation control specification. It does not by itself claim that every privacy or security control has already been implemented.

---

## 2. PRIVACY PRINCIPLE

Janavani should collect, process, display, or retain only information that is necessary for a clearly defined and verified purpose.

Citizens should not be encouraged to submit sensitive personal information merely to ask questions, understand a civic process, locate an office, provide feedback, or explore participation.

Privacy language must accurately describe implemented protections.

Janavani must not promise absolute confidentiality unless such protection is actually implemented and documented.

---

## 3. CONTACT SAFETY

Public contact pathways should warn citizens not to unnecessarily submit sensitive information.

Citizens should not be asked to provide through ordinary contact or feedback channels:

- Aadhaar numbers;
- passwords;
- financial credentials;
- authentication codes;
- private medical information;
- unnecessary government-identification data;
- other sensitive personal information.

If a particular workflow legitimately requires sensitive information in the future, the requirement, legal basis, security controls, storage rules, access controls, and user notice must be independently verified before implementation.

---

## 4. DATA MINIMISATION

Janavani should follow a data-minimisation principle.

For every proposed data field, determine:

1. Why is this information required?
2. Is it necessary for the stated purpose?
3. Can the purpose be achieved with less sensitive information?
4. Who needs access?
5. How long should it be retained?
6. Where is it stored?
7. What security controls apply?
8. What user notice is required?

Unnecessary collection must not be introduced merely because a technical system makes collection possible.

---

## 5. CITIZEN AGENCY PRINCIPLE

Janavani is intended to strengthen citizen understanding and agency.

The agency model is:

**JANAVANI ASSISTS**

**CITIZEN REVIEWS**

**CITIZEN DECIDES**

**CITIZEN ACTS**

Technology may help citizens understand information, identify relevant pathways, organise questions, or prepare for action.

The citizen remains responsible for reviewing information and deciding what action to take.

---

## 6. AI ASSISTANCE

AI may provide assistance where the relevant capability is verified and appropriately implemented.

AI assistance may include:

- explaining information;
- summarising documents;
- helping structure a question;
- helping identify potentially relevant civic pathways;
- assisting with navigation or information discovery;
- supporting multilingual interaction where verified.

AI output must be presented according to its actual capability and limitations.

AI must not be represented as an official government decision-maker, legal authority, or substitute for an authorised institution.

---

## 7. AI SAFETY BOUNDARY

**AI ASSISTANCE ≠ OFFICIAL DETERMINATION**

AI assistance must not be presented as:

- official government determination;
- guaranteed legal advice;
- guaranteed legal correctness;
- guaranteed institutional interpretation;
- guaranteed government action;
- guaranteed resolution of a citizen's problem.

Where official determination is required, citizens must be directed toward the appropriate official source or authority where that pathway is verified.

---

## 8. OFFICIAL DETERMINATION

Official determination belongs to the competent authority or institution responsible for the relevant matter.

Janavani may assist citizens in understanding information and preparing to engage with official systems, but it must not manufacture an official outcome.

The distinction between information assistance and official determination must remain visible in relevant user experiences.

---

## 9. CITIZEN REVIEW

Citizens should be encouraged to review important information before acting.

Where information may materially affect a citizen's rights, obligations, eligibility, application, complaint, or other civic action, Janavani should avoid presenting uncertain information as definitive.

Where appropriate, users should be encouraged to consult the relevant official source or authority.

---

## 10. CITIZEN DECISION

Janavani should not pressure citizens into a particular civic decision merely because an automated system recommends a pathway.

Recommendations, summaries, classifications, or suggested next steps must remain distinguishable from the citizen's own decision.

The interface should preserve user choice wherever the underlying workflow permits it.

---

## 11. CITIZEN ACTION

The purpose of assistance is to enable informed citizen action.

Janavani may help citizens:

- understand a process;
- identify relevant information;
- prepare questions;
- locate verified pathways;
- organise documents or facts where appropriate;
- communicate clearly with relevant institutions.

Janavani must not imply that using the platform guarantees government action, legal success, institutional response, or resolution.

---

## 12. PRIVACY AND AI

AI-enabled features must not expose sensitive personal information unnecessarily.

Before an AI feature is implemented, verify:

- what data is sent to the AI system;
- what data is retained;
- who can access it;
- whether the data is used for training or other secondary purposes;
- what deletion or retention controls exist;
- what user notice is provided;
- what security controls apply.

Do not claim a privacy or confidentiality property that has not been verified.

---

## 13. FORMS AND USER INPUT

Forms should request only information necessary for their stated purpose.

Every form should be reviewed for:

- purpose;
- required fields;
- optional fields;
- sensitive information;
- validation;
- accessibility;
- error handling;
- submission behaviour;
- storage;
- retention;
- user notice.

A form must not collect sensitive information simply because the field is technically available.

---

## 14. CONTACT / GET INVOLVED SAFETY

Contact and participation pathways must preserve the privacy principles established in the Contact / Get Involved Content Master.

Potential participants may include:

- Developers;
- Designers;
- Researchers;
- Legal / civic researchers;
- Writers;
- Students;
- Journalists;
- Volunteers;
- Civil-society organisations;
- Accessibility contributors;
- Documentation contributors;
- Community members.

These categories must not automatically be represented as a formal programme, employment opportunity, membership scheme, grant programme, or institutional partnership.

Participation must not require unnecessary sensitive personal information.

---

## 15. CHANNEL VERIFICATION

Privacy and agency protections apply across all public channels.

Before publication or implementation, verify:

- official contact channels;
- official social channels;
- external/community platforms;
- destination URLs;
- contribution pathways;
- data submitted through each pathway.

No invented links, invented accounts, invented partnerships, or unsupported response guarantees may be published.

Platform presence must not itself be interpreted as a formal partnership.

---

## 16. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE / RESEARCH

### 16.1 CURRENT

Only verified privacy controls, AI capabilities, data-handling practices, and citizen-facing functionality may be described as CURRENT.

### 16.2 BUILDING

Controls or capabilities currently being designed, reviewed, tested, or integrated must be identified as BUILDING.

### 16.3 FUTURE / RESEARCH

Prospective privacy controls, AI capabilities, integrations, or governance mechanisms must be identified as FUTURE / RESEARCH.

Planning material is not proof of deployment.

Future possibilities must never be presented as current implementation without verification.

Only verified capabilities may be described as current.

---

## 17. CLAIM CONTROL

Janavani must not invent or imply:

- absolute confidentiality;
- guaranteed privacy;
- guaranteed security;
- guaranteed legal correctness;
- guaranteed government action;
- guaranteed institutional outcomes;
- guaranteed response;
- guaranteed problem resolution;
- official government determination;
- government affiliation;
- formal partnership;
- unsupported AI capability.

Privacy and AI claims must be proportional to the controls and capabilities actually implemented.

---

## 18. ACCESSIBILITY AND AGENCY

Privacy and agency information must itself be accessible.

User-facing privacy and agency guidance should support:

- semantic HTML;
- keyboard accessibility;
- visible focus states;
- sufficient contrast;
- clear language;
- descriptive links;
- accessible form controls;
- mobile usability;
- multilingual considerations where implemented.

Important privacy or agency information must not be communicated only through colour, animation, or inaccessible interface elements.

---

## 19. PAGE BOUNDARIES

Privacy and citizen-agency principles apply across all six pages without replacing their individual responsibilities.

- Home explains what Janavani is and why it matters.
- How It Works explains the civic journey.
- Resources helps citizens understand and act.
- Vision explains where Janavani is going.
- About explains the project's origin, mission, values, and foundations.
- Contact / Get Involved explains how people can connect, contribute, or provide feedback.

The privacy and agency specification provides cross-site principles; it does not replace page-specific content masters.

---

## 20. IMPLEMENTATION GATE

Before implementing a privacy-sensitive or AI-assisted feature, verify:

- purpose and necessity;
- data minimisation;
- sensitive-data exposure;
- storage and retention;
- access controls;
- security controls;
- user notice;
- AI provider/data handling where applicable;
- citizen review and decision points;
- official determination boundaries;
- current/building/future status;
- accessibility;
- channel verification;
- documentation QA.

The current public website must remain untouched during the rebuild until the appropriate implementation and review gate is passed.

---

## 21. DOCUMENTATION / SOURCE AUTHORITY

Privacy and citizen-agency implementation should respect this authority order:

1. approved project architecture;
2. verified content masters;
3. verified asset inventory;
4. approved design system;
5. verified implementation requirements;
6. actual repository structure;
7. implementation details.

Planning notes, mock-ups, screenshots, or conversations must not override verified source documentation without an explicit project decision.

## 23. CONTENT STATUS

**Status:** Privacy and citizen-agency master specification established.

This document defines principles, boundaries, and implementation gates. It does not by itself certify the security, privacy, AI, data-processing, or confidentiality properties of any deployed system.

---

## 24. IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

No privacy-sensitive or AI-assisted feature should be treated as production-ready merely because it appears in:

- a design;
- a mock-up;
- a planning document;
- a prototype;
- a screenshot;
- a discussion;
- a test interface.

Production status requires verification of the actual implementation and its applicable controls.

---

## 25. FINAL PRIVACY / CITIZEN-AGENCY PRINCIPLE

Janavani should use technology to strengthen citizen understanding, judgment, and action while minimising unnecessary data collection, protecting users from avoidable privacy risks, and maintaining a clear boundary between AI assistance and official determination.

**JANAVANI ASSISTS**

**CITIZEN REVIEWS**

**CITIZEN DECIDES**

**CITIZEN ACTS**

**Technology is not the purpose of Janavani.**
