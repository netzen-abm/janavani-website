# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the canonical content responsibility of the Janavani How It Works page.

The How It Works page explains the operational civic journey through Janavani.

Its primary question is:

> **What actually happens when a citizen uses Janavani?**

The page must make the citizen journey understandable without pretending that every future capability is already implemented.

The page must explain:

```text
CITIZEN
   ↓
DESCRIBE
   ↓
UNDERSTAND
   ↓
FIND
   ↓
PREPARE
   ↓
COMMUNICATE
   ↓
FOLLOW UP
   ↓
CITIZEN REVIEW + DECISION
   ↓
LAWFUL CIVIC ACTION

The Home page introduces this journey.
How It Works explains it in operational depth.

# 2. HOW IT WORKS PRIMARY RESPONSIBILITY
The How It Works page must:
•	explain the citizen journey; 
•	explain what happens at each stage; 
•	distinguish citizen responsibility from Janavani assistance; 
•	explain where information, evidence, documents, and pathways may be involved; 
•	explain current capability truthfully; 
•	distinguish building and future capabilities; 
•	explain AI assistance without presenting AI as authority; 
•	explain privacy and safety considerations; 
•	explain evidence and provenance; 
•	explain failure and degraded paths; 
•	explain what Janavani does not guarantee; 
•	provide appropriate pathways to Resources and other pages; 
•	and make the workflow understandable visually. 
The page must not become:
•	a technical architecture document; 
•	a complete legal manual; 
•	a government-service directory; 
•	a promise of institutional outcomes; 
•	a detailed description of every future Janavani subsystem; 
•	an AI product advertisement; 
•	or a duplicate of the Home page.

# 4. CITIZEN ENTRY
The journey begins with the citizen's public problem, concern, question, or need.
The citizen should be able to describe the issue in ordinary language.
The system should not require the citizen to understand government terminology before beginning.
The conceptual entry point is:
CITIZEN HAS A PUBLIC PROBLEM
            ↓
CITIZEN DESCRIBES THE CONCERN
            ↓
JANAVANI HELPS STRUCTURE THE PROBLEM
The citizen remains the source of the original concern.
Janavani may help interpret, classify, organize, retrieve, or structure information.
It must not silently change the citizen's underlying meaning.
Where uncertainty exists, the system should make that uncertainty visible rather than inventing certainty.
________________________________________
# 5. STAGE 1 — DESCRIBE
Primary question
What happened?
The citizen describes the public problem or concern in ordinary language.
Examples may include:
•	a public service problem; 
•	difficulty accessing an institution; 
•	uncertainty about responsibility; 
•	a procedural problem; 
•	a document or information problem; 
•	a public-system communication issue; 
•	or another civic concern. 
The citizen does not need to begin with a perfect legal or administrative description.
Janavani's role may include helping structure the description.
The system must preserve citizen agency.
The goal of this stage is:
ORDINARY-LANGUAGE CONCERN
        ↓
STRUCTURED PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
The page should communicate that citizens begin with their lived problem, not with technical system terminology.
________________________________________
# 6. STAGE 2 — UNDERSTAND
Primary question
What kind of public-system issue is this?
Janavani may help the citizen understand the nature of the problem.
This can include:
•	identifying the broad category of issue; 
•	identifying relevant concepts; 
•	finding useful public information; 
•	explaining terminology; 
•	identifying possible procedural context; 
•	distinguishing known information from assumptions; 
•	and identifying questions that require further verification. 
The purpose is not to produce an automatic official determination.
The relationship is:
CITIZEN CONCERN
      ↓
INFORMATION + CONTEXT
      ↓
BETTER UNDERSTANDING
AI may assist with understanding, classification, retrieval, translation, or document processing where appropriate.
AI output must remain distinguishable from authoritative sources.
________________________________________
# 7. STAGE 3 — FIND
Primary question
Where should the citizen go, communicate, or act?
Janavani may help identify:
•	relevant offices; 
•	authorities; 
•	departments; 
•	procedures; 
•	official information; 
•	available channels; 
•	documents; 
•	or other appropriate civic pathways. 
Where an office or pathway cannot be verified, Janavani must not present an invented answer as fact.
The desired relationship is:
PROBLEM
   ↓
RESPONSIBILITY / PATHWAY
   ↓
RELEVANT AUTHORITY OR CHANNEL
Information should be sourced and, where appropriate, linked to authoritative material.
The system must distinguish:
•	verified information; 
•	inferred information; 
•	citizen-provided information; 
•	AI-generated assistance; 
•	and unresolved uncertainty. 
________________________________________
# 8. STAGE 4 — PREPARE
Primary question
What information or document is appropriate for the next step?
Janavani may help the citizen organize information and prepare materials for review.
Possible assistance may include:
•	structuring the problem; 
•	organizing evidence; 
•	identifying relevant documents; 
•	preparing a draft communication; 
•	organizing factual information; 
•	identifying missing information; 
•	formatting information for a selected pathway; 
•	or preparing a document that the citizen can review. 
Generated material must never be presented as automatically legally correct.
The citizen must review important content before acting.
The relationship is:
INFORMATION
    ↓
STRUCTURE
    ↓
DRAFT / DOCUMENT
    ↓
CITIZEN REVIEW
The preparation stage must preserve the distinction between assistance and authority.
________________________________________
# 9. STAGE 5 — COMMUNICATE
Primary question
How can the citizen communicate through the appropriate civic pathway?
The citizen may choose to communicate with an appropriate institution or authority.
Janavani may assist with:
•	preparing a communication; 
•	organizing the facts; 
•	identifying the intended recipient; 
•	identifying an appropriate channel; 
•	preparing supporting information; 
•	or explaining what the citizen should review before submission. 
The website must not imply that Janavani automatically submits every communication.
It must also not imply that preparing a communication guarantees:
•	delivery; 
•	acceptance; 
•	acknowledgement; 
•	investigation; 
•	action; 
•	resolution; 
•	or government response. 
The distinction is:
JANAVANI PREPARES / ASSISTS
             ↓
CITIZEN REVIEWS
             ↓
CITIZEN DECIDES
             ↓
APPROPRIATE COMMUNICATION
________________________________________
# 10. STAGE 6 — FOLLOW UP
Primary question
What happens after the communication or civic action?
Where supported, Janavani may help citizens:
•	retain records; 
•	understand next steps; 
•	organize responses; 
•	track relevant information; 
•	prepare follow-up communications; 
•	review what has happened; 
•	and remain engaged with the civic process. 
Follow-up capability must be described according to verified implementation.
The website must not imply that Janavani automatically tracks every government response unless that capability is actually implemented and verified.
The conceptual model is:
ACTION
  ↓
RECORD
  ↓
OBSERVE
  ↓
REVIEW
  ↓
FOLLOW UP WHERE APPROPRIATE
Follow-up is part of citizen participation, not a guarantee of institutional accountability.
________________________________________
# 11. CITIZEN REVIEW AND DECISION
Citizen agency remains central throughout the entire journey.
The fundamental model is:
JANAVANI ASSISTS
       ↓
CITIZEN REVIEWS
       ↓
CITIZEN DECIDES
       ↓
CITIZEN ACTS
Janavani does not become the citizen's authority.
The citizen remains responsible for deciding whether and how to act.
The website must not imply that:
•	AI output is automatically correct; 
•	Janavani makes governmental decisions; 
•	Janavani determines legal rights; 
•	Janavani replaces lawful institutions; 
•	Janavani replaces qualified legal representation; 
•	a generated document is automatically legally correct; 
•	or an attempted communication is automatically delivered. 
The page should make this relationship visible rather than hiding it in fine print.
________________________________________
# 12. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE
How It Works must clearly distinguish present capability from development and future direction.
## 12.1 CURRENT
A capability may be described as current only when it is:
1.	implemented; 
2.	verified in the repository; 
3.	and available to users in the relevant deployment. 
Current public claims must therefore be checked against actual implementation.
Planning documents alone are not evidence of current capability.
The page must never present an unverified planned feature as an operational feature.
________________________________________
## 12.2 BUILDING
A capability actively under development may be described as:
•	being built; 
•	currently under development; 
•	part of the current build; 
•	or an area of active development. 
It must not be presented as already operational.
Appropriate wording includes:
Janavani is building...
This capability is currently under development.
The current build is focused on...
________________________________________
# 12.3 FUTURE / RESEARCH
Long-term or exploratory capabilities may be presented as future possibilities or research directions.
These may include:
•	advanced civic intelligence; 
•	evidence infrastructure; 
•	governance analytics; 
•	privacy-preserving technologies; 
•	decentralized systems; 
•	resilient communications; 
•	community-owned infrastructure; 
•	advanced AI infrastructure; 
•	and other strategic directions documented elsewhere in the Janavani knowledge base. 
Future technologies must remain clearly identified as future or research.

# 13. AI POSITION
AI is assistance infrastructure.
It is not the authority of the civic process.
AI may assist with:
•	understanding; 
•	classification; 
•	document processing; 
•	retrieval; 
•	translation; 
•	multimodal analysis; 
•	drafting; 
•	structured workflows; 
•	and other appropriate support functions. 
The permanent distinction is:
AI ASSISTANCE
      ≠
OFFICIAL DETERMINATION
No individual AI model or provider should be treated as the sole source of truth.
Where appropriate, critical workflows should retain:
•	deterministic paths; 
•	human review; 
•	manual paths; 
•	verified-source paths; 
•	or degraded paths. 
The website must communicate AI capability truthfully.
It must not imply that an AI-generated answer is automatically:
•	legally authoritative; 
•	factually complete; 
•	institutionally binding; 
•	or guaranteed to produce an outcome.

# 14. PRIVACY AND SAFETY
Privacy and safety are architectural principles.
They are not decorative website features.
A citizen may provide:
•	personal information; 
•	evidence; 
•	documents; 
•	photographs; 
•	location information; 
•	communications; 
•	identity-related information; 
•	or information concerning other people. 
The How It Works page should explain that data handling must respect principles including:
•	Privacy by Design; 
•	Privacy by Default; 
•	Safety by Design; 
•	Safety by Default; 
•	data minimisation; 
•	purpose limitation; 
•	identity minimisation; 
•	user control; 
•	evidence protection; 
•	provenance; 
•	access control; 
•	retention discipline; 
•	auditability; 
•	and abuse prevention. 
The detailed specification belongs primarily in:
12-PRIVACY-AND-CITIZEN-AGENCY.md
How It Works should explain the operational implications without duplicating the entire privacy document.

# 15. EVIDENCE AND PROVENANCE
Information used during a civic journey should remain distinguishable by source and status.
Where relevant, the system should distinguish:
AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE
        ↓
VERIFIED REPOSITORY INFORMATION
        ↓
SYSTEM-DERIVED INFORMATION
        ↓
CITIZEN-PROVIDED INFORMATION
        ↓
EXPERT ANALYSIS
        ↓
AI ASSISTANCE
These categories must not silently become interchangeable.
Evidence should be treated carefully.
The website must not imply that a citizen-provided document has been independently verified merely because it has been uploaded or processed.
Similarly, AI-generated interpretation must not be presented as authoritative evidence without appropriate verification.
________________________________________
# 16. FAILURE AND DEGRADED PATHS
A trustworthy civic system must explain what happens when the preferred path does not work.
Possible failures include:
•	insufficient information; 
•	ambiguous citizen description; 
•	uncertain classification; 
•	unavailable office information; 
•	missing official information; 
•	unavailable integration; 
•	failed document generation; 
•	failed communication; 
•	unavailable service; 
•	technical outage; 
•	or an unsupported request. 
The system should prefer transparent degradation over invented certainty.
The principle is:
UNCERTAINTY
    ↓
DISCLOSE
    ↓
OFFER AVAILABLE ALTERNATIVE
    ↓
PRESERVE CITIZEN CONTROL
If an automated capability fails, the citizen should not be misled into believing that the action succeeded.
The website must not hide important failures behind reassuring language.
________________________________________
# 17. WHAT JANAVANI DOES NOT GUARANTEE
The How It Works page must clearly state that Janavani does not guarantee:
•	a government response; 
•	acceptance of a complaint; 
•	investigation; 
•	correction of a public problem; 
•	legal success; 
•	institutional action; 
•	delivery of every communication; 
•	correctness of every generated output; 
•	availability of every government pathway; 
•	or a particular civic outcome. 
Janavani assists citizens in navigating public problems.
It does not control the institutions to which citizens communicate.
The distinction is:
JANAVANI CAN ASSIST
        ↓
CITIZEN CAN ACT
        ↓
INSTITUTION RESPONDS OR DOES NOT RESPOND
        ↓
OUTCOME CANNOT BE GUARANTEED BY JANAVANI
This is a trust principle, not a disclaimer added merely for legal protection.

# 22. CLAIM CONTROL
The How It Works page must not invent or imply unsupported:
•	user numbers; 
•	success rates; 
•	government response rates; 
•	impact statistics; 
•	partnerships; 
•	certifications; 
•	deployment claims; 
•	institutional relationships; 
•	technical capabilities; 
•	integration claims; 
•	automated-delivery claims; 
•	or outcomes. 
If a claim cannot be verified, it must not be presented as current fact.
Particular care must be taken with statements such as:
•	"Janavani sends your complaint"; 
•	"Janavani contacts the government"; 
•	"Janavani tracks your case"; 
•	"Janavani identifies the correct authority"; 
•	"AI verifies your documents"; 
•	"your complaint will be resolved." 
These statements require implementation-level verification before being presented as current capabilities.
