## 2. REFERENCE CLASSIFICATION

### CANONICAL

Material that defines an approved current Janavani principle, identity, architecture, governance rule, or public position.

May be used directly when consistent with the current repository and higher-authority documents.

### CURRENT PRODUCT

Material describing capabilities verified as currently implemented and available to users.

Must be checked against the actual repository before being presented as operational.

### CURRENT ECOSYSTEM DIRECTION

Material describing the intended current ecosystem scope and development direction.

It may describe capabilities that are part of the ecosystem target but are not yet implemented.

Such material must not be written as an existing capability unless implementation is verified.

### FUTURE / RESEARCH

Ideas, technologies, experiments, or long-term capabilities under consideration.

Examples include:

- decentralised identity
- zero-knowledge proofs
- Nostr
- Nym
- Reticulum
- Freenet
- blockchain
- community nodes
- decentralised storage
- advanced AI infrastructure
- Bhu-Janavani
- governance intelligence

These must be clearly presented as future, research, exploratory, or potential capabilities.

### HISTORICAL / SUPERSEDED

Older planning, architecture, implementation, or audit material.

Useful for understanding project evolution but not authoritative for current direction unless explicitly adopted again.

### REFERENCE ONLY

Material that may provide ideas, examples, terminology, research direction, or explanatory context but should not automatically become Janavani policy or public claims.

## 9. PRIVACY AND CITIZEN AGENCY REFERENCES

### 9.1 Privacy and Citizen Agency Material

**Classification:** CANONICAL PRINCIPLE / ARCHITECTURAL REFERENCE

### Core principles

- Privacy by Design
- Privacy by Default
- Safety by Design
- Safety by Default
- minimum necessary collection
- purpose limitation
- identity minimisation
- explicit consent where required
- user review before consequential actions
- evidence protection
- provenance
- access control
- retention discipline
- auditability
- abuse prevention

### Why privacy matters

Civic participation can involve information that is:

- personally sensitive
- politically sensitive
- legally significant
- socially sensitive
- capable of exposing a citizen to unwanted attention or retaliation

Privacy therefore supports citizen agency.

The objective is not secrecy. The objective is accountable civic infrastructure that protects people while preserving appropriate evidence, provenance, lawful process and transparency.

### Counter-arguments that must be addressed where relevant

#### “Transparency requires collecting more information.”

Transparency and unnecessary collection are not identical.

Janavani should seek the minimum information needed for the stated civic purpose while preserving appropriate provenance and accountability.

#### “Government records are public, so privacy is irrelevant.”

Public availability does not automatically justify unlimited aggregation, profiling, redistribution, or retention.

Context and purpose still matter.

#### “More identity information makes civic action more trustworthy.”

Identity may sometimes be necessary, but unnecessary identity exposure can create risk.

Janavani should minimise identity where the workflow permits.

#### “AI needs more data to work better.”

Technical convenience is not by itself sufficient justification for collecting personal information.

AI capability must remain purpose-bound and subject to appropriate privacy controls.

#### “Privacy makes accountability harder.”

The objective is not secrecy.

The objective is accountable civic infrastructure that protects people while preserving evidence, provenance, lawful process and appropriate transparency.

#### “Citizens can simply choose not to use Janavani.”

Meaningful citizen agency requires informed choice.

Privacy should therefore be a property of the system rather than something citizens must sacrifice merely to participate.

### Website placement

Primary treatment:

- **About:** why privacy and citizen agency are part of Janavani's identity.
- **How It Works:** how privacy considerations affect evidence, identity, review and action.
- **Resources:** deeper explanation of privacy, safety, evidence and provenance.
- **Vision:** privacy-preserving civic infrastructure as a long-term design principle.

Home should communicate the principle briefly rather than becoming a privacy-policy page.

4. Identify the concepts it contributes.
5. Identify the pages it may inform.
6. Identify any current/future claim risk.
7. Verify implementation before making capability claims.

9. Update the relevant content master if the reference changes approved content.

## 22. CONFLICT RESOLUTION

If two references disagree:

1. Check the current authority hierarchy.
2. Check the actual repository implementation.
3. Check the date and status of each reference.
4. Determine whether one reference is historical or superseded.
5. Preserve the historical record.
6. Do not silently merge conflicting claims.
7. Record the resolution when it materially affects website content.

The goal is not to erase historical disagreement.

The goal is to prevent historical ambiguity from becoming public misinformation.
