# JANAVANI — 15-SEO-AND-METADATA-STANDARDS

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the master standards for Janavani's search visibility, machine-readable understanding, answer visibility, entity understanding, metadata, content discoverability, and public-facing information architecture.

The scope includes:

- SEO — Search Engine Optimisation;
- AEO — Answer Engine Optimisation;
- GEO — Generative Engine Optimisation;
- entity and knowledge-graph discoverability;
- structured data and machine-readable metadata;
- crawlability and indexability;
- internal linking;
- multilingual and internationalisation considerations;
- accessibility as a discoverability foundation;
- social/share metadata;
- canonical and URL standards;
- search-result presentation;
- AI-readable content;
- measurement and change control.

These standards improve discoverability and comprehension without allowing optimisation to distort Janavani's citizen-centred purpose or overstate current capabilities.

---

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

SEO, AEO, GEO, and related optimisation systems are communication disciplines, not substitutes for useful content.

Janavani should optimise for:

1. citizen understanding;
2. factual accuracy;
3. clear information architecture;
4. trustworthy source signals;
5. machine-readable meaning;
6. accessibility;
7. discoverability;
8. useful answers;
9. accurate entity recognition;
10. sustainable search visibility.

Search visibility must never take priority over truthful communication.

---

## 3. SEO — SEARCH ENGINE OPTIMISATION

SEO covers the technical and content practices that help search engines discover, crawl, understand, and appropriately rank Janavani content.

SEO standards include:

- crawlable HTML content;
- meaningful page titles;
- useful meta descriptions;
- one clear H1 per page;
- logical H2/H3 hierarchy;
- descriptive URLs;
- meaningful internal links;
- appropriate canonical URLs;
- valid robots directives;
- XML sitemap where appropriate;
- accurate image alternative text;
- fast and usable pages;
- mobile usability;
- structured data where appropriate;
- no deceptive keyword stuffing;
- no hidden text intended to manipulate rankings.

SEO should support the actual information architecture rather than create artificial content.

---

## 4. AEO — ANSWER ENGINE OPTIMISATION

AEO focuses on making useful information easy for answer systems to identify, interpret, and present in response to user questions.

Janavani should structure important information so that common questions can be answered clearly.

AEO practices include:

- direct answers to important citizen questions;
- concise definitions;
- clear question-and-answer sections where appropriate;
- descriptive headings;
- explicit relationships between concepts;
- authoritative source references where relevant;
- consistent terminology;
- factual and bounded statements;
- clear distinction between current and future capabilities.

AEO must not be used to manufacture answers that Janavani cannot substantiate.

---

## 5. GEO — GENERATIVE ENGINE OPTIMISATION

GEO concerns the ability of generative AI and retrieval-augmented systems to understand, retrieve, cite, summarise, and accurately represent Janavani information.

GEO standards include:

- clear source-oriented content;
- consistent terminology;
- explicit factual statements;
- strong contextual headings;
- meaningful page relationships;
- machine-readable metadata;
- structured data where appropriate;
- stable URLs;
- clear authorship or organisational identity where appropriate;
- transparent current/building/future status;
- avoidance of contradictory versions of important claims.

The objective is not to manipulate generative systems into mentioning Janavani.

The objective is to make Janavani information sufficiently clear, accurate, structured, and traceable that systems can represent it correctly.

---

## 6. ENTITY SEO AND KNOWLEDGE-GRAPH READINESS

Janavani should be represented as a distinct, consistently named entity.

Entity-oriented standards include:

- consistent organisation/project name;
- consistent description;
- consistent official domain;
- consistent logo where applicable;
- consistent social/profile references where verified;
- consistent relationship between Janavani and its pages;
- accurate organisational/contact information;
- structured data identifying the appropriate entity type where applicable.

No invented entity relationships, partnerships, affiliations, government connections, awards, statistics, or institutional endorsements should be introduced for search visibility.

---

## 7. SEARCH INTENT AND CITIZEN QUESTIONS

Content should be designed around real information needs rather than arbitrary keyword lists.

Important intent categories may include:

- understanding what Janavani is;
- understanding how Janavani works;
- finding civic resources;
- understanding a civic process;
- finding a relevant official pathway;
- learning how to participate;
- understanding the project's vision;
- contacting or contributing to Janavani.

Search-oriented content must preserve the distinction between Janavani's assistance and official institutional determination.

---

## 8. CONTENT QUALITY AND INFORMATION GAIN

Pages should provide substantive information rather than merely repeating keywords.

Content should:

- answer the page's primary question;
- add useful context;
- avoid unnecessary repetition;
- use precise terminology;
- distinguish facts from possibilities;
- identify important limitations;
- link to deeper authoritative information;
- remain understandable to ordinary citizens.

Optimisation must not create thin pages whose primary purpose is ranking.

---

## 9. PAGE-LEVEL SEO / AEO / GEO BOUNDARY

### HOME

Primary role:

- explain what Janavani is;
- explain why it matters;
- establish the primary entity and purpose;
- provide clear pathways to deeper pages.

Search and answer optimisation should reinforce identity and purpose.

### HOW IT WORKS

Primary role:

- explain the civic journey;
- answer process-related questions;
- distinguish assistance, citizen review, citizen decision, and official determination.

### RESOURCES

Primary role:

- provide discoverable civic information and resource pathways;
- organise resources clearly;
- support targeted information needs.

### VISION

Primary role:

- explain future direction and research possibilities;
- clearly label prospective capabilities.

### ABOUT

Primary role:

- explain origin, mission, foundations, values, and verified project context.

### CONTACT / GET INVOLVED

Primary role:

- provide verified ways to connect, contribute, or provide feedback;
- never imply an unverified active channel or partnership.

---

## 10. TITLE TAG STANDARDS

Every indexable page should have a unique, descriptive title.

Titles should:

- identify the page's primary subject;
- use natural language;
- avoid unnecessary keyword repetition;
- remain understandable when displayed independently;
- preserve the Janavani identity where appropriate.

Titles must not claim capabilities or affiliations that are not verified.

---

## 11. META DESCRIPTION STANDARDS

Where applicable, each important page should have a useful meta description.

Descriptions should:

- accurately summarise the page;
- reflect its primary purpose;
- use natural language;
- help users understand what they will find;
- avoid unsupported promises;
- avoid keyword stuffing.

Meta descriptions are presentation aids, not a place for hidden claims.

---

## 12. URL AND CANONICAL STANDARDS

Public URLs should be:

- stable;
- descriptive;
- predictable;
- readable;
- lowercase where practical;
- free from unnecessary parameters;
- consistent with the site's information architecture.

Canonical URLs should identify the preferred public version of materially equivalent content.

Avoid unnecessary duplicate URLs for the same page.

URL changes should be documented and redirected appropriately where required.

---

## 13. ROBOTS, SITEMAP, AND CRAWLABILITY

The site should maintain appropriate technical discovery controls.

Verify:

- robots.txt behaviour;
- XML sitemap availability where appropriate;
- canonical declarations;
- index/noindex directives;
- crawlable navigation;
- absence of accidental blocking;
- correct status codes;
- accessible page resources.

Technical controls must not accidentally hide important public content from discovery.

Private or non-public material should not be made indexable merely for visibility.

---

## 14. STRUCTURED DATA

Structured data may be used where it accurately represents visible or otherwise appropriate page information.

Potential schema categories should be selected according to actual content and eligibility, such as:

- Organization;
- WebSite;
- WebPage;
- BreadcrumbList;
- Article where genuinely applicable;
- FAQPage only where appropriate and supported by actual page content;
- other relevant schema types only when justified.

Structured data must:

- match the actual page;
- not contain fabricated claims;
- not mark future capabilities as current;
- not create misleading rich-result signals;
- remain technically valid.

Structured data is supporting metadata, not a substitute for human-readable content.

---

## 15. SEMANTIC HTML AND MACHINE READABILITY

Semantic HTML supports both accessibility and machine interpretation.

Use appropriate elements such as:

- header;
- nav;
- main;
- section;
- article;
- footer;
- headings;
- lists;
- buttons;
- links;
- forms;
- labels.

Important information should not exist only inside images, animations, canvas elements, or inaccessible interface states.

---

## 16. INTERNAL LINKING

Internal links should establish meaningful relationships between pages.

Important relationships include:

- HOME → HOW IT WORKS;
- HOW IT WORKS → RESOURCES;
- RESOURCES → relevant deeper information;
- VISION → future/research context;
- ABOUT → project foundations;
- CONTACT / GET INVOLVED → verified participation pathways.

Anchor text should describe the destination.

Avoid vague link labels when a more descriptive label is practical.

Internal linking must not create artificial networks of repetitive links solely for ranking.

---

## 17. EXTERNAL SOURCES AND AUTHORITY

Where Janavani explains civic or institutional information, relevant authoritative external sources should be used where appropriate.

External references must be:

- relevant;
- accurate;
- traceable;
- current where time-sensitive;
- clearly distinguished from Janavani itself.

Do not imply endorsement, partnership, or institutional authority merely because Janavani links to an organisation.

---

## 18. MULTILINGUAL SEO / AEO / GEO

Multilingual content must preserve meaning across languages.

Consider:

- language declarations;
- translated titles;
- translated descriptions;
- language-specific URLs or routing where applicable;
- appropriate hreflang implementation where justified;
- script-specific typography;
- longer translated text;
- translated headings;
- translated structured content;
- consistent terminology.

Machine translation must not silently alter:

- legal meaning;
- citizen rights or responsibilities;
- current/building/future status;
- official determination boundaries;
- privacy or safety language.

---

## 19. ACCESSIBILITY AS DISCOVERABILITY

Accessibility and discoverability reinforce one another.

Maintain:

- semantic HTML;
- meaningful alternative text;
- descriptive links;
- keyboard accessibility;
- visible focus states;
- sufficient contrast;
- accessible headings;
- accessible forms;
- mobile usability;
- reduced-motion support where appropriate.

Accessibility requirements must not be sacrificed for visual SEO or interface effects.

---

## 20. SOCIAL AND SHARE METADATA

Where social sharing is supported, verify:

- Open Graph metadata;
- social title;
- social description;
- preview image;
- canonical URL;
- platform-specific metadata where justified.

Preview content must accurately represent the destination.

No social metadata should imply an unverified partnership, programme, capability, or achievement.

---

## 21. IMAGE AND MEDIA DISCOVERABILITY

Images and media should have meaningful contextual relationships with their surrounding content.

Where appropriate:

- descriptive filenames;
- meaningful alt text;
- width/height information;
- responsive image handling;
- suitable formats;
- captions where useful;
- accurate surrounding text;
- structured media metadata where justified.

Decorative imagery should not be used to create artificial keyword signals.

---

## 22. CORE WEB PERFORMANCE AND TECHNICAL QUALITY

Search visibility should not be pursued independently of usable performance.

Verify:

- mobile usability;
- loading performance;
- stable layout;
- responsive behaviour;
- efficient asset loading;
- valid HTML where practical;
- correct status codes;
- absence of broken links;
- absence of accidental blocking.

Performance optimisation must not remove necessary accessibility or explanatory content.

---

## 23. SEARCH RESULT AND AI REPRESENTATION SAFETY

Janavani must not optimise in ways that cause systems to infer:

- official government status without verification;
- government endorsement without verification;
- institutional partnership without verification;
- live functionality that is still building;
- future capability as current;
- guaranteed outcomes;
- official legal or administrative authority.

Search snippets, structured data, page titles, headings, social previews, and AI-readable content are all public claim surfaces.

---

## 24. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE STATUS

SEO, AEO, GEO, and structured data must follow the status framework.

### CURRENT

Only verified current capabilities may be represented as currently available.

### BUILDING

Capabilities under development or integration must be represented with appropriate status language.

### FUTURE / RESEARCH

Prospective capabilities must be clearly represented as future, conceptual, experimental, or research-oriented.

Planning material is not proof of deployment.

A search result, AI answer, screenshot, diagram, or structured-data entry is not proof of implementation.

---

## 25. MEASUREMENT AND QA

SEO/AEO/GEO QA should review, where applicable:

- indexability;
- crawlability;
- titles;
- descriptions;
- headings;
- canonical URLs;
- structured data;
- internal links;
- broken links;
- sitemap;
- robots controls;
- page performance;
- accessibility;
- multilingual implementation;
- search-result presentation;
- AI-readable factual consistency;
- entity consistency;
- current/building/future claims.

Metrics should be interpreted as signals, not as proof of citizen value.

---

## 26. CHANGE CONTROL

Review this standard when:

- page structure changes;
- URLs change;
- major content changes;
- new languages are added;
- new integrations are introduced;
- structured data changes;
- current/building/future status changes;
- search or AI representation changes materially;
- privacy or accessibility requirements change.

Important public claims should be rechecked after material changes.

---

## 27. IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

This document defines SEO, AEO, GEO, entity, metadata, machine-readability, and discoverability standards.

It does not certify that the current website satisfies these standards.

Implementation must follow:

1. verified content masters;
2. verified asset inventory;
3. verified design system;
4. verified shared infrastructure;
5. verified privacy and citizen-agency principles;
6. verified NOW / BUILDING / FUTURE status;
7. actual repository evidence.

The current public website must remain untouched during the rebuild until the appropriate implementation and review gate is passed.

---

Before publication, verify:

- titles are unique and accurate;
- meta descriptions are accurate where used;
- H1/H2/H3 hierarchy is logical;
- URLs are stable and descriptive;
- canonical controls are correct;
- robots controls are intentional;
- sitemap is correct where applicable;
- internal links are meaningful;
- structured data matches page content;
- entity information is consistent;
- external references are verified;
- multilingual metadata is appropriate;
- accessibility requirements are preserved;
- social metadata is accurate;
- images are accessible and discoverable;
- performance is acceptable;
- SEO claims are truthful;
- AEO content is answerable and bounded;

- no optimisation technique creates unsupported claims.

Optimisation should increase discoverability without increasing claims beyond the evidence.
