# JANAVANI — SIX-PAGE CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

**Status:** Active  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Document:** `02-SIX-PAGE-CONTENT-ARCHITECTURE.md`  
**Purpose:** Define the permanent information architecture, page responsibilities, content boundaries, navigation logic, and rebuild sequence for the six-page Janavani public website.

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the six primary pages of the Janavani website and establishes what each page is responsible for communicating.

The objective is to create one coherent civic information system rather than six unrelated pages.

The architecture must prevent:

- unnecessary duplication
- unclear page roles
- content sprawl
- conflicting explanations
- future/current capability confusion
- navigation ambiguity
- repeated technical explanations
- excessive card-based presentation

The six pages are:

1. Home
2. How It Works
3. Resources
4. Vision
5. About
6. Contact / Get Involved

---

## 2. CORE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE

Each concept should have one primary home.

A concept may be introduced briefly on another page when necessary, but its complete explanation belongs on its designated primary page.

The website should feel like one connected system:

```text
                         JANAVANI
                            │
              ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
              ↓             ↓             ↓
            WHAT?          WHY?          HOW?
            HOME           ABOUT     HOW IT WORKS
              │             │             │
              └─────────────┼─────────────┘
                            │
                 ┌──────────┴──────────┐
                 ↓                     ↓
             LEARN / EXPLORE       WHERE IT IS GOING
             RESOURCES                VISION
                                        │
                                        ↓
                                  PARTICIPATE
                              CONTACT / GET INVOLVED
```

The visitor should never need to understand the internal architecture of the project in order to understand the website.

## 3. SIX-PAGE ARCHITECTURE

| Page | Primary responsibility |
|---|---|
| Home | Constitutional premise, citizen agency, Janavani purpose, orientation |
| How It Works | Citizen journey and operational explanation |
| Resources | Civic knowledge, references, explanations and deeper material |
| Vision | Larger destination and current/building/future distinction |
| About | Identity, principles, architecture, privacy and citizen agency |
| Contact / Get Involved | Communication, participation, contribution and collaboration |

---

## 4. HOME

### Primary question

> **Why does Janavani exist, what is it, and why should a citizen care?**

### Primary responsibility

Home establishes:

- the constitutional premise
- citizen agency
- Janavani's purpose
- the high-level citizen journey
- the distinction between citizen and technology
- a clear path into the rest of the website

### Constitutional opening

The Home page begins with:

> **THE REPUBLIC BEGINS WITH THE PEOPLE**

> **WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA** having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC**

Janavani begins from the constitutional premise that citizens are not spectators of governance. They are participants in the Republic.

### Intended progression

```text
Constitutional premise
        ↓
Citizen agency
        ↓
Why Janavani exists
        ↓
What Janavani helps with
        ↓
High-level civic journey
        ↓
How It Works
        ↓
Resources / deeper understanding
        ↓
Participation
```

### Home must not become

- an operational manual
- a complete technical architecture document
- a privacy policy
- a long research catalogue
- a future-technology catalogue
- a contact directory

Home provides orientation and meaning.

## 5. HOW IT WORKS

### Primary question

> **What happens when a citizen brings a real civic problem to Janavani?**

### Primary responsibility

How It Works explains the operational civic journey.

### Core journey

```text
Citizen Reality
        ↓
Understanding
        ↓
Evidence / Context
        ↓
Correct Authority
        ↓
Lawful Civic Action
        ↓
Submission / Communication
        ↓
Government Response
        ↓
Tracking
        ↓
Follow-up / RTI / Escalation
        ↓
Outcome
        ↓
Feedback / Accountability
```

### How It Works should explain

- what information a citizen provides
- how the problem is understood
- how evidence/context matters
- how the relevant authority is identified
- how lawful civic action is prepared
- how communication/submission works
- what happens after submission
- how tracking and follow-up work
- where human review remains important
- what Janavani assists with
- what remains the citizen's decision

### Citizen-agency model

The preferred model is:

```text
JANAVANI ASSISTS
CITIZEN REVIEWS
CITIZEN DECIDES
CITIZEN ACTS
```

### Boundary with Home

Home introduces the journey.

How It Works explains the journey in operational depth.

How It Works must not simply repeat Home.

## 6. RESOURCES

### Primary question

> **Where can a citizen learn more?**

### Primary responsibility

Resources provides deeper civic, legal, technical, documentary, and explanatory material.

### Resource categories may include

- civic knowledge
- constitutional references
- government-process explanations
- evidence and provenance
- privacy and safety
- AI explanations
- decentralised technology research
- architecture explanations
- useful documents
- source references
- FAQs
- terminology and glossary material
- verified external references

### Future technology placement

Technologies such as:

- Nostr
- Nym
- Freenet
- Reticulum
- blockchain
- zero-knowledge proofs
- decentralised identity
- verifiable credentials
- decentralised storage
- advanced AI infrastructure

must be clearly classified when they are not current implementation.

Resources may explain them without implying that Janavani currently operates them.

### Resources must not become

- a second Home page
- an uncontrolled archive
- an unverified technology catalogue
- a place for unsupported capability claims

## 7. VISION

### Primary question

> **Where is Janavani going?**

### Primary responsibility

Vision explains the larger destination of Janavani and distinguishes:

```text
CURRENT
    ↓
BUILDING
    ↓
FUTURE / RESEARCH
```

### Vision should address

- participatory democracy
- citizen participation beyond voting
- accountability
- long-term civic infrastructure
- community participation
- privacy-preserving infrastructure
- future ecosystem direction
- governance intelligence
- possible decentralised infrastructure
- Bhu-Janavani as a future direction where appropriate

### Claim boundary

Future capability must not be presented as current.

Planning material is not proof of deployment.

Screenshot is not proof of deployment.

Prototype is not proof of deployment.

Source-code presence is not proof of deployment.

### Vision must not become

- a list of promises
- a product roadmap without context
- a claim that research systems are already deployed
- an architectural specification replacing the technical repository

## 12. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE CONTENT BOUNDARY

Every capability-related statement must be classified.

### CURRENT

Implemented and verified as available to users.

### BUILDING

An active development target whose implementation is underway or formally planned.

### FUTURE / RESEARCH

Strategic, exploratory, experimental, or long-term capability.

### Public claim rule

Do not publish a future capability as current.

The following are not sufficient proof of deployment:

- planning documents
- screenshots
- prototypes
- source-code presence
- design mockups
- conversation statements

Production claims require deployment evidence and runtime verification.

## 13. PRIVACY AND CITIZEN AGENCY

Privacy is a cross-site principle, but each page has a different role.

### Home

Briefly communicate that citizen agency includes protection of personal information and safe participation.

### How It Works

Explain how privacy affects:

- evidence
- identity
- review
- consequential actions
- submission

### Resources

Provide deeper explanations of:

- Privacy by Design
- Privacy by Default
- Safety by Design
- Safety by Default
- minimum necessary collection
- purpose limitation
- identity minimisation
- provenance
- evidence protection

### About

Explain why privacy is part of Janavani's identity and architecture.

### Vision

Explain privacy-preserving civic infrastructure as a long-term principle.

## 14. VISUAL INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

The site should not present every section as a card.

The visual system should prefer:

```text
IDEA
  ↓
VISUAL EXPLANATION
  ↓
3–5 meaningful elements
  ↓
TAKEAWAY
  ↓
NEXT ACTION
```

Use meaningful visual storytelling where it improves comprehension.

Appropriate visual structures include:

- citizen journey diagrams
- process flows
- accountability pathways
- ecosystem diagrams
- current/building/future architecture
- privacy/agency relationships
- civic evidence pathways

Avoid:

- decorative statistics
- invented metrics
- repetitive card grids
- unnecessary visual effects
- visual clutter

The target character is:

**Civic + intelligent + trustworthy + modern + human + restrained.**

## 15. NAVIGATION LOGIC

Primary navigation should make the six-page architecture understandable immediately.

Recommended conceptual order:

```text
Home
How It Works
Resources
Vision
About
Get Involved
```

The exact visual placement may evolve with the design system, but the six-page information architecture remains stable unless a documented change is approved.

Every page should provide meaningful pathways to the next relevant page.

## 17. SEO / AEO / GEO ARCHITECTURE

Each page should have a distinct search and answer purpose.

### Home

Entity + purpose + citizen agency.

### How It Works

Questions about the civic workflow and citizen journey.

### Resources

Explanatory and reference-oriented queries.

### Vision

Long-term direction, participatory democracy, civic infrastructure and future architecture.

### About

Identity, principles, privacy, architecture and trust.

### Contact / Get Involved

Participation, contribution and communication pathways.

Do not create duplicate search intent merely to increase page count.

---

## 18. ACCESSIBILITY ARCHITECTURE

Each page must maintain:

- semantic HTML
- logical heading hierarchy
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- meaningful link text
- meaningful ALT text
- sufficient contrast
- usable controls
- accessible navigation
- responsive text
- accessible forms where applicable

The information architecture must remain understandable without relying solely on visual styling.

---

## 19. MULTILINGUAL ARCHITECTURE

Translation must preserve the six-page information hierarchy.

Translated content must not:

- destroy heading hierarchy
- create duplicate controls
- break navigation
- hide critical content
- create unusable layouts

Translation availability is not evidence that every translated phrase has been independently verified.

---

## 20. SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE BOUNDARY

The six HTML pages are developed and verified individually before shared infrastructure is standardized.

Shared infrastructure:

- `styles.css`
- `script.js`
- `gtranslation.js`

Required sequence:

```text
Six fresh pages
        ↓
Individual page verification
        ↓
Shared infrastructure integration
        ↓
Six-page regression testing
        ↓
Final release QA
```

Do not repeatedly modify shared infrastructure to compensate for unfinished page content.

---

## 21. REBUILD SEQUENCE

The fresh rebuild follows this controlled sequence:

```text
Documentation foundation
        ↓
Six-page content architecture
        ↓
Page content masters
        ↓
Home
        ↓
How It Works
        ↓
Resources
        ↓
Vision
        ↓
About
        ↓
Contact / Get Involved
        ↓
Individual verification of each page
        ↓
Shared CSS / JS / translation integration
        ↓
Six-page regression
        ↓
Final QA
        ↓
Production backup
        ↓
Release authorization
        ↓
Production replacement
        ↓
Post-deployment verification
```

The existing public website remains protected during this process.

---

## 22. CONTENT PRESERVATION RULE

The rebuild preserves useful content and principles while removing accumulated implementation debt.

Preserve:

- constitutional foundation
- citizen-agency philosophy
- civic journey
- privacy and safety principles
- verified product knowledge
- meaningful visual assets
- useful research references

Do not preserve merely because it exists:

- duplicated HTML structures
- obsolete CSS patches
- encoding damage
- contradictory claims
- obsolete implementation assumptions
- unsupported future claims
- accidental terminal text

Content preservation and code preservation are separate decisions.

## 24. CHANGE CONTROL

Any material change to the six-page architecture must document:

- change ID
- date
- affected page(s)
- reason
- new responsibility
- removed responsibility
- duplication impact
- navigation impact
- SEO/AEO/GEO impact
- accessibility impact
- verification

## 25. FINAL ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE

The six pages are not six separate websites.

They form one civic information system:

```text
THE REPUBLIC
      ↓
THE PEOPLE
      ↓
CITIZEN AGENCY
      ↓
UNDERSTANDING
      ↓
LAWFUL ACTION
      ↓
ACCOUNTABILITY
      ↓
PARTICIPATION
```

Technology exists to strengthen this relationship.

The website should always make the citizen's role clearer, not more dependent on the technology.

**Technology is not the purpose of Janavani.**
