# JANAVANI — WEBSITE PROJECT PRINCIPLES

**Status:** Active — Foundation Principle  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 26 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Permanent principles governing the content, design, implementation, verification, and maintenance of the Janavani public website.

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the principles that must guide every significant Janavani website decision.

It exists to ensure that the website remains aligned with the broader Janavani citizen-governance ecosystem while maintaining a clear boundary between:

- the website;
- the wider ecosystem;
- current implementation;
- active development;
- future capabilities;
- research;
- and historical material.

These principles apply to content, information architecture, visual design, accessibility, SEO, technical implementation, public claims, and future maintenance.

---

# 2. WEBSITE ROLE

The Janavani website is a public communication and access surface of the broader Janavani ecosystem.

It is not the ecosystem itself.

```text
JANAVANI ECOSYSTEM
        ↓
Multiple independent interfaces
        ↓
Shared capabilities and contracts
        ↓
JANAVANI WEBSITE
Public web interface

The website must communicate the ecosystem accurately without claiming that every ecosystem capability is already implemented.
The website must never become the architectural definition of Janavani.
________________________________________
# 3. CITIZEN AGENCY
Janavani exists to strengthen citizen capability and agency.
The website must communicate citizens as active participants in public life rather than passive recipients of government services.
The fundamental relationship is:
JANAVANI ASSISTS
       ↓
CITIZEN REVIEWS
       ↓
CITIZEN DECIDES
       ↓
CITIZEN ACTS
Janavani must not position itself as the citizen's authority.
The website must not imply that Janavani:
•	makes governmental decisions; 
•	replaces public institutions; 
•	guarantees government action; 
•	guarantees a government response; 
•	replaces qualified legal representation; 
•	determines legal rights; 
•	determines official government outcomes; 
•	or acts with public authority on behalf of the citizen without appropriate authorization. 
________________________________________
# 4. CONSTITUTIONAL AND CIVIC FOUNDATION
Janavani operates within India's constitutional and legal environment.
The website may communicate the citizen-centered purpose of Janavani through appropriate constitutional and civic principles.
The Preamble's:
We, the People of India
framing is central to the citizen-agency orientation of the project.
Where relevant, website content may discuss constitutional principles associated with:
•	equality; 
•	freedom; 
•	life and personal liberty; 
•	privacy; 
•	civic participation; 
•	Fundamental Duties; 
•	and accountable democratic governance. 
Constitutional text, statutory law, judicial decisions, authoritative government information, system-derived information, citizen-provided information, expert analysis, and AI assistance must remain distinguishable.
Janavani is not:
•	a court; 
•	a government authority; 
•	an election authority; 
•	a law-enforcement body; 
•	or a substitute for qualified legal representation.

# 5. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE DISTINCTION
Every significant public statement about Janavani capabilities must have a clear status.
## 5.1 CURRENT
A capability may be described as current only when:
1.	it is implemented; 
2.	it has been verified in the repository; 
3.	and it is available to users in the relevant deployment. 
Current claims must be supported by implementation evidence.
________________________________________
## 5.2 BUILDING
A capability is building when it is actively being developed but is not yet available as a verified public capability.
Appropriate language includes:
•	Janavani is building... 
•	Currently being developed... 
•	The current build is focused on... 
•	This capability is under development. 
Building capabilities must not be represented as already operational.
________________________________________
## 5.3 FUTURE / RESEARCH
Future or research capabilities are strategic, exploratory, experimental, or long-term directions.
Examples may include:
•	decentralised identity; 
•	verifiable credentials; 
•	zero-knowledge proofs; 
•	Nostr; 
•	Nym; 
•	Reticulum; 
•	Freenet; 
•	blockchain; 
•	decentralised storage; 
•	community-owned infrastructure; 
•	resilient communications; 
•	mesh communications; 
•	satellite-supported communication; 
•	advanced AI infrastructure; 
•	governance intelligence; 
•	Bhu-Janavani; 
•	government performance analytics; 
•	citizen dashboards; 
•	department scorecards; 
•	governance heat maps. 
These must not be presented as existing Janavani capabilities unless implementation is independently verified.

# 6. IMPLEMENTATION TRUTH
The website must remain truthful about what the system actually does.
The implementation hierarchy is:
PUBLIC CLAIM
     ↓
DOCUMENTED INTENT
     ↓
ACTUAL IMPLEMENTATION
     ↓
VERIFICATION
     ↓
DEPLOYED AVAILABILITY
Planning documents do not prove implementation.
A design concept does not prove implementation.
A prototype does not automatically prove public availability.
An AI-generated answer does not prove a government fact.
An attempted transmission does not prove successful delivery.
The website must reflect verified reality rather than aspirational assumptions.
________________________________________
# 7. AI PRINCIPLE
AI is optional, purpose-bound, and replaceable infrastructure.
AI may assist with capabilities such as:
•	understanding; 
•	classification; 
•	document processing; 
•	retrieval; 
•	translation; 
•	multimodal analysis; 
•	drafting; 
•	structured workflows; 
•	and other appropriately bounded tasks. 
However:
AI ASSISTANCE
      ≠
OFFICIAL DETERMINATION
The website must not imply that an AI model is:
•	the source of truth for government information; 
•	a government authority; 
•	a legal authority; 
•	infallible; 
•	or capable of guaranteeing an outcome. 
No single AI model or provider should become an architectural single point of failure.
Where appropriate, critical workflows should have:
•	deterministic paths; 
•	human review; 
•	manual paths; 
•	or truthful degraded operation. 
________________________________________
# 8. PRIVACY PRINCIPLE
Privacy is an architectural principle, not merely a website policy statement.
Janavani follows:
•	Privacy by Design; 
•	Privacy by Default; 
•	data minimisation; 
•	purpose limitation; 
•	identity minimisation; 
•	user control; 
•	evidence protection; 
•	provenance; 
•	access control; 
•	retention discipline; 
•	auditability; 
•	and abuse prevention. 
The website must avoid unnecessary collection and unnecessary exposure of citizen information.
Public content must explain privacy in a way that supports citizen understanding and agency.
________________________________________
# 9. SAFETY PRINCIPLE
Safety is treated as an architectural requirement.
Janavani follows:
•	Safety by Design; 
•	Safety by Default; 
•	consequential-action review; 
•	abuse prevention; 
•	evidence protection; 
•	appropriate access controls; 
•	truthful capability communication; 
•	and failure-aware design. 
The website must not encourage citizens to treat automated outputs as authoritative without appropriate review.
________________________________________
# 10. EVIDENCE AND PROVENANCE
Where Janavani processes evidence, documents, photographs, records, or other information, provenance and source distinction are important.
The website should preserve the distinction between:
CITIZEN-PROVIDED INFORMATION
        ↓
SYSTEM-DERIVED INFORMATION
        ↓
EXTERNAL AUTHORITATIVE INFORMATION
        ↓
EXPERT ANALYSIS
        ↓
AI-ASSISTED OUTPUT
These categories must not be silently represented as equivalent.
The existence of evidence does not automatically establish the legal or factual conclusion that a citizen may believe follows from it.
________________________________________
# 11. FAILURE-ISOLATION PRINCIPLE
The website should be designed as one interface within an ecosystem capable of operating independently from other surfaces where technically appropriate.
The broader ecosystem principle is:
ONE FAILURE
    ↓
SHOULD NOT
    ↓
BECOME SYSTEM-WIDE FAILURE
Examples include:
•	Web unavailable → other interfaces may continue. 
•	Telegram unavailable → Web/mobile/API may continue. 
•	AI unavailable → deterministic or manual paths may continue where appropriate. 
•	RAG unavailable → the system must not invent facts. 
•	Vision capability unavailable → manual evidence paths may remain available where practical. 
•	Blockchain unavailable → ordinary civic workflows continue. 
The website must not imply dependency on an optional technology unless that dependency actually exists.
________________________________________
# 12. CONTENT PRINCIPLE
Website content must be:
•	accurate; 
•	understandable; 
•	purposeful; 
•	citizen-centered; 
•	evidence-aware; 
•	appropriately scoped; 
•	honest about limitations; 
•	and consistent with current documentation. 
Content should answer the user's actual question rather than merely advertise technology.
The website should prefer:
CLARITY
    ↓
UNDERSTANDING
    ↓
CAPABILITY
    ↓
ACTION
over unnecessary promotional language.
________________________________________
# 13. EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
The six pages form one editorial system.
Each page must have a distinct responsibility.
HOME
Why does Janavani matter?

HOW IT WORKS
How does the civic journey work?

RESOURCES
What can I learn or use?

VISION
Where is Janavani going?

ABOUT
What is Janavani founded on?

GET INVOLVED
How can I participate?
A concept should have one primary home.
Cross-page links may continue a concept, but pages should not become duplicates of one another.
________________________________________

# 15. DESIGN PRINCIPLE
The website should feel:
•	civic; 
•	intelligent; 
•	trustworthy; 
•	human; 
•	modern; 
•	restrained; 
•	editorial; 
•	purposeful; 
•	and premium without becoming ornamental. 
It should not feel like:
•	a generic startup landing page; 
•	a government portal clone; 
•	an AI advertisement; 
•	a collection of repetitive cards; 
•	or a technology showcase without civic meaning. 
Visual design must serve communication.
________________________________________
# 16. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE
Information architecture must reduce cognitive load.
Users should be able to understand:
1.	what Janavani is; 
2.	why it exists; 
3.	what they can currently do; 
4.	how the civic journey works; 
5.	what information is available; 
6.	what is being built; 
7.	and what belongs to the future. 
Navigation should reflect the six-page architecture rather than exposing internal implementation complexity.
________________________________________
# 17. SEO AND ACCESSIBILITY PRINCIPLE
SEO and accessibility are part of the website architecture.
Each page should have:
•	a unique title; 
•	useful metadata; 
•	appropriate canonical URL; 
•	semantic headings; 
•	meaningful internal links; 
•	meaningful image ALT text; 
•	a clear Janavani entity description; 
•	and appropriate structured data where justified. 
Accessibility should include:
•	semantic HTML; 
•	keyboard navigation; 
•	readable contrast; 
•	visible focus states; 
•	meaningful labels; 
•	appropriate ARIA only where necessary; 
•	responsive layouts; 
•	reduced-motion considerations; 
•	and meaningful text alternatives.

# 19. REFERENCE AUTHORITY
Website development must use documented sources with explicit authority.
The primary website knowledge base is:
JANAVANI-WEBSITE-DOCUMENTATION/
Supporting reference material is:
JANAVANI-WEBSITE-DOCUMENTATION/
└── 90-REFERENCE-SOURCES/
    ├── CURRENT-REFERENCES/
    ├── HISTORICAL-REFERENCES/
    └── BACKUPS/
Reference material informs development but does not automatically override the current knowledge base.
The broader Janavani ecosystem repository remains authoritative for ecosystem-level architecture and governance.
The actual implementation repository remains authoritative for whether a capability exists.
________________________________________
# 20. HISTORICAL MATERIAL
Historical material is valuable project memory.
However, historical material must not automatically become a current requirement.
Older:
•	website designs; 
•	architecture plans; 
•	content maps; 
•	developer guides; 
•	implementation plans; 
•	audits; 
•	backups; 
•	and experiments 
must be interpreted according to their status.
Historical material should be retained where useful, but clearly separated from current authority.
________________________________________
# 21. DOCUMENTATION CHANGE PRINCIPLE
A significant website decision must not remain only in conversation.
The required lifecycle is:
DECISION
   ↓
DOCUMENTATION
   ↓
IMPLEMENTATION
   ↓
VERIFICATION
   ↓
CHANGELOG
When a significant principle or architectural decision changes:
1.	update the relevant documentation; 
2.	update the implementation if required; 
3.	verify the implementation; 
4.	record the change in 19-CHANGELOG.md. 
________________________________________

# 23. NO INVENTED CLAIMS
The website must not invent:
•	statistics; 
•	user counts; 
•	success rates; 
•	government response rates; 
•	impact numbers; 
•	partnerships; 
•	certifications; 
•	deployment status; 
•	technical capabilities; 
•	institutional relationships; 
•	or other factual claims. 
If a number or claim cannot be supported, it should not be presented as fact.
________________________________________
# 24. FUTURE-ORIENTED BUT PRESENT-TRUTHFUL
Janavani may communicate an ambitious long-term ecosystem.
The website should not hide the future direction.
However, ambition must remain clearly separated from present capability.
The correct relationship is:
WHAT EXISTS
     ↓
WHAT IS BEING BUILT
     ↓
WHAT MAY COME NEXT
A strong vision does not require pretending that future capabilities already exist.
________________________________________

THE REPUBLIC

# 27. FINAL RULE
When a proposed website change creates uncertainty, the developer must resolve the uncertainty through documented authority rather than assumption.

The order of preference is:
CURRENT CANONICAL DOCUMENTATION
        ↓
VERIFIED REPOSITORY IMPLEMENTATION
        ↓
APPROVED PROJECT DECISION
        ↓
REFERENCE MATERIAL
        ↓
NEW PROPOSAL

The website should never silently convert an assumption, experiment, historical idea, or future possibility into a current Janavani claim.

Citizen agency, implementation truth, privacy, safety, and clear separation between present capability and future ambition are permanent principles of the Janavani website.
