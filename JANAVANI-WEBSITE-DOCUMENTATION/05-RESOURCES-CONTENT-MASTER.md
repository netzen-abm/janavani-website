# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the canonical content responsibility of the Janavani Resources page.

The Resources page answers:

> **What can a citizen learn, understand, use, or reference?**

Resources is the public civic knowledge layer of the Janavani website.

It should help citizens understand:

- public systems;
- civic pathways;
- relevant concepts;
- documents and procedures;
- citizen rights and responsibilities;
- practical civic action;
- frequently asked questions;
- technology concepts relevant to Janavani;
- privacy and safety concepts;
- and trustworthy external references.

The page must prioritize understanding over information volume.

The fundamental relationship is:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
UNDERSTANDING
   ↓
USEFUL INFORMATION
   ↓
PRACTICAL PATHWAY
   ↓
INFORMED CITIZEN ACTION

Resources is a knowledge layer.
It is not the operational Janavani workflow.

# 2. RESOURCES PRIMARY RESPONSIBILITY
The Resources page must:
•	provide useful civic explanations; 
•	organize knowledge into understandable categories; 
•	help citizens find relevant information; 
•	provide practical guides where appropriate; 
•	explain important terminology; 
•	provide FAQs; 
•	provide reusable civic resources; 
•	explain relevant technologies in accessible language; 
•	distinguish authoritative sources from explanatory material; 
•	distinguish current Janavani capability from research or future technology; 
•	provide links to authoritative external sources where appropriate; 
•	support citizen learning and agency; 
•	and provide pathways into the deeper Janavani ecosystem. 
The page must not become:
•	a duplicate of Home; 
•	a duplicate of How It Works; 
•	a legal advice service; 
•	an unofficial government directory; 
•	a technology marketing page; 
•	a collection of random external links; 
•	a repository of unsupported claims; 
•	or a catalogue of every technology considered by Janavani.

# 5. CIVIC UNDERSTANDING
Resources should help citizens understand public systems without requiring prior technical or administrative knowledge.
Possible topics include:
•	how public institutions work; 
•	roles and responsibilities; 
•	common civic terminology; 
•	public procedures; 
•	administrative pathways; 
•	forms and documents; 
•	communication channels; 
•	evidence and records; 
•	escalation concepts; 
•	appeals and follow-up; 
•	and general civic participation. 
Explanations should begin with the citizen's question.
Prefer:
What does this mean for a citizen?
over:
What technical terminology does the system use?
The purpose is practical understanding.

# 6. PUBLIC SYSTEMS
Where appropriate, Resources may explain how citizens can understand public-system structures.
Examples include:
•	identifying the relevant institution; 
•	understanding departmental responsibility; 
•	understanding procedural pathways; 
•	finding official information; 
•	understanding forms; 
•	understanding required documents; 
•	distinguishing central, state, and local responsibilities; 
•	and recognizing when information requires verification. 
The page must not invent institutional relationships.
If a government office, authority, department, or pathway is presented as current information, the information should be verified against an authoritative source.
________________________________________
# 7. CITIZEN ACTION
Resources may provide practical guidance for lawful civic participation.
Examples may include:
•	preparing questions; 
•	organizing facts; 
•	preserving records; 
•	understanding a public notice; 
•	preparing a representation; 
•	understanding a complaint process; 
•	understanding information-request mechanisms; 
•	preparing follow-up questions; 
•	documenting communication; 
•	and reviewing responses. 
Resources should support citizen agency.
The relationship remains:
JANAVANI ASSISTS
       ↓
CITIZEN REVIEWS
       ↓
CITIZEN DECIDES
       ↓
CITIZEN ACTS
Resources must not imply that Janavani decides for the citizen.

# 11. EVIDENCE AND VERIFICATION
Resources should help citizens distinguish information by source and status.
Where relevant, distinguish:
AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE
        ↓
VERIFIED INFORMATION
        ↓
SYSTEM-DERIVED INFORMATION
        ↓
CITIZEN-PROVIDED INFORMATION
        ↓
EXPERT ANALYSIS
        ↓
AI ASSISTANCE
These categories must not silently become interchangeable.
A citizen-provided document is not automatically independently verified.
An AI-generated explanation is not automatically authoritative.
A reference link is not automatically an endorsement.
Verification should be encouraged where accuracy matters.

# 13. EXTERNAL TECHNOLOGY REFERENCES
External technologies and organizations must be described neutrally unless a formal Janavani relationship has been verified.
Do not casually state:
•	Janavani recommends; 
•	Janavani endorses; 
•	Janavani partners with; 
•	Janavani uses; 
•	Janavani depends on. 
unless the corresponding claim is verified.
Prefer language such as:
•	Reference; 
•	External reference; 
•	Learn about; 
•	Research direction; 
•	Technology being evaluated; 
•	Relevant technical concept. 
External references are not automatically Janavani dependencies.

# 14. CURRENT / BUILDING / FUTURE
Resources must preserve the project's capability-status distinction.
## 14.1 CURRENT
A capability may be described as current only when it is:
1.	implemented; 
2.	verified in the repository; 
3.	and available to users in the relevant deployment. 
Planning documents are not evidence of current capability.
________________________________________
## 14.2 BUILDING
A capability actively under development may be described as:
•	currently being developed; 
•	part of the current build; 
•	under development; 
•	or being built. 
It must not be presented as operational.
________________________________________
## 14.3 FUTURE / RESEARCH
A strategic, exploratory, experimental, or long-term technology may be presented as:
•	future; 
•	research; 
•	exploration; 
•	long-term direction; 
•	or possibility. 
Future technology must never silently become a current Janavani capability.

# 26. RESOURCE SELECTION RULE
Before adding a resource, ask:
1.	Is it useful to citizens? 
2.	Is its source identifiable? 
3.	Is its status clear? 
4.	Is it current? 
5.	Does it belong on Resources? 
6.	Is it duplicated elsewhere? 
7.	Does it require verification? 
8.	Could it be misunderstood as an endorsement? 
9.	Could it be mistaken for current Janavani capability? 
10.	Does it strengthen citizen understanding or agency? 
If the answer to these questions is unclear, the resource requires review before publication.

   ↓

   ↓

   ↓
