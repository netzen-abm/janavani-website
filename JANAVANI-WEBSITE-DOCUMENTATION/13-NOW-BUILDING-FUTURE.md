# JANAVANI — 13-NOW-BUILDING-FUTURE

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the master framework for distinguishing what Janavani is doing NOW, what Janavani is BUILDING, and what belongs to the FUTURE / RESEARCH horizon.

The purpose is to prevent planned capabilities, prototypes, concepts, architectural ideas, screenshots, or discussions from being presented as deployed functionality.

Status language must remain evidence-based and must be updated when implementation status changes.

---

## 2. STATUS PRINCIPLE

Every significant Janavani capability should have an explicit status:

- CURRENT
- BUILDING
- FUTURE / RESEARCH

Status is a claim about implementation maturity.

A capability must not be classified as CURRENT merely because:

- it has been discussed;
- it appears in a roadmap;
- it appears in a design;
- a mock-up exists;
- a prototype exists;
- code has been written;
- a screenshot exists;
- a planning document describes it;
- the capability works in an isolated test.

Current status requires verification of the actual relevant implementation.

---

## 3. CURRENT

CURRENT means the capability has been verified as functioning in the relevant public or approved implementation context.

A CURRENT capability should have:

- an identifiable implementation;
- a verified user pathway where applicable;
- verified supporting infrastructure;
- accurate documentation;
- appropriate accessibility consideration;
- appropriate claim language;
- no material contradiction between documentation and actual behaviour.

CURRENT does not mean perfect, complete, or permanently available.

If a CURRENT capability changes, becomes unavailable, or is replaced, its status must be reviewed.

---

## 4. BUILDING

BUILDING means the capability is actively being designed, developed, integrated, tested, reviewed, or prepared for implementation.

BUILDING may include:

- code under development;
- integration work;
- interface implementation;
- testing;
- data or infrastructure preparation;
- documentation preparation;
- staged rollout work.

BUILDING capabilities must not be described to the public as fully operational unless the relevant implementation has been verified.

A prototype or test environment may demonstrate progress, but it is not automatically evidence of production deployment.

---

## 5. FUTURE / RESEARCH

FUTURE / RESEARCH means the capability is prospective, conceptual, exploratory, experimental, or dependent on future decisions or infrastructure.

Examples may include:

- future AI capabilities;
- advanced civic-data integration;
- new institutional integrations;
- prospective automation;
- research concepts;
- future multilingual capabilities;
- future analytics;
- future citizen-service workflows.

Future ideas may be described as possibilities, research directions, or planned areas of exploration.

They must never be presented as current implementation without verification.

---

## 6. EVIDENCE STANDARD

Status claims should be supported by the strongest available evidence.

Evidence may include:

1. verified production behaviour;
2. verified repository implementation;
3. verified integration and deployment state;
4. documented testing results;
5. approved implementation records;
6. design or prototype evidence;
7. planning or research documentation.

The lower levels of evidence must not be used to make stronger deployment claims than they support.

In particular:

**Planning material is not proof of deployment.**

**A polished interface is not proof of deployment.**

**A screenshot is not proof of deployment.**

**A prototype is not automatically a production system.**

---

## 7. STATUS LABELING

Where status is relevant, Janavani should use clear language.

### CURRENT

Use language such as:

- currently available;
- currently implemented;
- verified capability;
- available through the verified pathway.

### BUILDING

Use language such as:

- currently being developed;
- being tested;
- under integration;
- being prepared for implementation.

### FUTURE / RESEARCH

Use language such as:

- future possibility;
- research direction;
- under consideration;
- prospective capability;
- future development area.

Avoid ambiguous wording that makes a future capability appear operational.

---

## 8. PUBLIC WEBSITE CLAIM CONTROL

The public website must not overstate Janavani's present capabilities.

Public content should distinguish:

- what citizens can use now;
- what is being built;
- what is being explored.

A future roadmap must not be written in a way that implies all listed capabilities already exist.

Visual design must follow the same rule.

A diagram, animation, screenshot, or illustration can communicate a capability claim even when no explicit text says that the capability is live.

---

## 9. AI STATUS CONTROL

AI capabilities require particularly careful status control.

AI may be:

- CURRENT where the actual AI-assisted workflow is verified;
- BUILDING where the AI workflow is being developed or integrated;
- FUTURE / RESEARCH where the capability is conceptual or prospective.

AI assistance must not be presented as official government determination.

The distinction remains:

**JANAVANI ASSISTS**

**CITIZEN REVIEWS**

**CITIZEN DECIDES**

**CITIZEN ACTS**

AI capability status must also be consistent with the Privacy and Citizen Agency specification.

---

## 10. INTEGRATION STATUS

External integrations should be classified according to their actual state.

Examples include:

- Telegram;
- GitHub;
- translation services;
- government or civic data sources;
- document services;
- communication channels;
- analytics;
- AI providers.

The existence of an account, repository, API, connector, or test integration does not by itself prove that a public-facing capability is deployed.

Each integration must be independently verified before being described as CURRENT.

---

## 11. DOCUMENTATION STATUS

Documentation may describe:

- CURRENT implementation;
- BUILDING work;
- FUTURE / RESEARCH direction.

Documentation itself must not be treated as proof of implementation.

When implementation changes, the relevant documentation should be reviewed so that status descriptions remain accurate.

Contradictions between actual implementation and documentation should be treated as a QA issue.

---

## 12. DESIGN AND VISUAL STATUS

Design work must preserve status distinctions.

The following can accidentally communicate deployment:

- interface screenshots;
- dashboard mock-ups;
- process diagrams;
- feature cards;
- animated demonstrations;
- sample conversations;
- system architecture diagrams;
- application previews.

If such material represents a non-current capability, it should be clearly labelled as:

- concept;
- prototype;
- example;
- planned;
- future;
- research.

Visual polish must never substitute for deployment evidence.

---

## 13. SIX-PAGE APPLICATION

Status distinctions apply across all six public pages.

### HOME

Home should communicate verified current value while clearly distinguishing building and future directions where appropriate.

### HOW IT WORKS

The civic journey should describe verified current pathways and clearly identify any building or future workflow.

### RESOURCES

Resources should distinguish available resources from planned or future resource systems.

### VISION

Vision may discuss future possibilities, research directions, and long-term development, but must not imply that prospective capabilities are already deployed.

### ABOUT

About may explain the project's development and foundations while keeping historical, current, and future claims distinct.

### CONTACT / GET INVOLVED

Contact / Get Involved may describe current participation pathways and future opportunities, but must not present unverified programmes, partnerships, or channels as active.

---

## 14. CLAIM CONTROL

Janavani must not invent or imply:

- current functionality that is not verified;
- current deployment that has not been verified;
- official government integration that has not been verified;
- formal institutional partnerships that have not been verified;
- active programmes that have not been verified;
- active social or contact channels that have not been verified;
- statistics that have not been verified;
- guaranteed outcomes;
- unsupported AI capability.

When uncertain, use a weaker accurate status rather than a stronger unsupported claim.

---

## 15. CHANGE CONTROL

Status must be reviewed when:

- a feature moves from prototype to production;
- a feature is deployed;
- a feature is withdrawn;
- an integration changes;
- a public pathway changes;
- a capability is materially modified;
- a privacy or security control changes;
- a public claim becomes outdated.

Status changes should be documented before public claims are updated.

---

## 16. VERIFICATION GATE

Before a capability is described as CURRENT, verify:

- actual implementation;
- deployment state;
- user-accessible pathway where applicable;
- supporting infrastructure;
- relevant integrations;
- accessibility;
- privacy/security requirements;
- documentation;
- public claim wording.

If these cannot be verified, the capability should remain BUILDING or FUTURE / RESEARCH as appropriate.

---

## 17. SOURCE AUTHORITY

NOW / BUILDING / FUTURE status decisions should respect this authority order:

1. verified actual implementation;
2. verified deployment/integration state;
3. approved project architecture;
4. verified content masters;
5. verified asset inventory;
6. approved design system;
7. verified implementation requirements;
8. planning and research material.

Planning documents and discussions may inform future work but do not override verified implementation evidence.

---

## 18. QA CHECKLIST

Before publication or implementation, verify:

- CURRENT claims are supported by actual evidence;
- BUILDING claims are clearly labelled;
- FUTURE / RESEARCH claims are clearly labelled;
- no prototype is presented as production;
- no screenshot is presented as deployment evidence;
- no mock-up is presented as a live feature;
- no future diagram is presented as current architecture;
- AI status is accurate;
- integrations are verified;
- public channels are verified;
- government or institutional claims are verified;
- privacy/security claims are verified;
- accessibility requirements are preserved;
- page-specific boundaries remain intact.

---

## 19. IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

This document defines status language and evidence requirements.

It does not certify any specific Janavani capability as CURRENT.

Actual status must be established through the relevant implementation, deployment, integration, and QA evidence.

The current public website must remain untouched during the rebuild until the appropriate implementation and review gate is passed.

---

## 20. CONTENT STATUS

**Status:** NOW / BUILDING / FUTURE master specification established.

This document is a governance and claim-control specification.

It should be reviewed whenever significant Janavani capabilities or deployment states change.

---

## 21. FINAL NOW / BUILDING / FUTURE PRINCIPLE

Janavani should communicate honestly about where it stands: what citizens can use now, what is actively being built, and what belongs to the future or research horizon.

The distinction protects citizen trust and prevents technology, design, planning, or aspiration from being mistaken for verified implementation.

**JANAVANI ASSISTS**

**CITIZEN REVIEWS**

**CITIZEN DECIDES**

**CITIZEN ACTS**

**Technology is not the purpose of Janavani.**
